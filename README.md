# embodied-labor-audit
Quantitative audit of independent artistic labor, intermediary asymmetry, and burnout volatility (Python / Matplotlib)
#### Auditing Embodied Artistic Labor: A Quantitative Self-Study

**Author:** Carolina Reyes Velázquez
**Domain:** Cultural Economy · Critical Data Analysis · Creative Labor Sustainability 

#### Overview

Independent musicians operating in the live event and hospitality sectors often lack transparent metrics regarding how their physical labor translates into economic sustainability. Between 2021 and early 2026, I built a lightweight personal data pipeline—integrating **Google Calendar**, **mobile Google Forms** for real-time expense logging, and a **relational CRM**—to audit the real operational structure of freelance performance work. 

Rather than using these metrics solely for revenue optimization, this analysis served as an empirical risk assessment. It revealed how hourly embodied labor concentrates physical risk (where a single injury or accident threatens total livelihood) while dispersing effort across low-margin intermediaries. 

#### Key Analytical Findings

*(All financial figures are normalized to percentages to preserve client and personal confidentiality; sample size: n = 33 tracked performance/booking records).* 

1. **Intermediary Labor Asymmetry:** 

  * **78.8%** of total event volume flowed through third-party agencies, generating **62.4%** of total income.
  * **15.2%** of bookings were direct or semi-direct, yet accounted for **37.6%** of total income—demonstrating how intermediary structures absorb disproportionate physical effort.
2. **Burnout Volatility (Pareto Concentration):** 

  * **33.3%** of events consisted of repetitive baseline hotel residencies generating only **12.5%** of total income.
  * **64.1%** of total income depended on just **24.2%** of events (high-stress seasonal spikes such as New Year's Eve and Valentine's Day, reaching up to four stacked performances in a single day).
3. **Hidden Operational Overhead:** 

  * Mobility and venue logistics accounted for **31.0%** of structural operational costs, followed by digital/studio base (**25.0%**) and musical equipment/audio maintenance (**24.0%**).

#### Conclusion & Career Pivot

While the data highlighted clear levers to increase short-term profitability, it also exposed the long-term unsustainability of hourly physical performance without institutional safety nets. Based on this empirical audit, I made the evidence-based decision to close this commercial chapter in March 2026 and transition toward academic research in **musicology, human-centered data systems, and generative AI**.
